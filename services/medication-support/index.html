Medication Support and RN-Delegated Care

Magnolia Senior Care supports residents with medication-related needs based on their individual assessments and care plans. When medication administration is required, appropriately qualified caregivers may perform specific medication tasks under authorization and delegation from a Registered Nurse, in accordance with Washington State requirements. Our team also coordinates with prescribing healthcare professionals and pharmacies as needed to support safe, consistent medication routines.

How it works

What families can expect.

Level 1

Independent self-administration

Some residents manage their own medications. When their assessment supports it, they continue to do so, and the arrangement is documented in their care plan.

Level 2

Medication assistance

For residents who can still direct their own medications but need help, caregivers may provide assistance allowed under Washington rules, such as reminders, opening containers, or handing a medication to the resident. The resident remains the person taking the medication.

Level 3

Medication administration

When a resident can no longer self-administer, medications must be given by a legally authorized practitioner, such as a licensed nurse, or by a caregiver acting under valid nurse delegation, unless another lawful arrangement applies. Caregivers do not make clinical medication decisions; prescription changes come from the resident's prescriber.

Nurse delegation

Specific, individual, and RN-approved

Nurse delegation is specific to one resident, one caregiver, and one authorized task. The delegating Registered Nurse decides whether a task is appropriate to delegate, and the caregiver must meet Washington's training, competency, documentation, and supervision requirements. Not every medication, injection, or nursing procedure can be delegated.

Coordination

Prescribers and pharmacies

Magnolia coordinates with prescribing healthcare professionals and pharmacies as needed, including refills and medication changes, and keeps families informed.

Washington references: WAC 388-76-10440, WAC 388-76-10455, WAC 246-840-910 through 246-840-970 (nurse delegation).

RN-Led Clinical Excellence + Family-Centered Care

The role of RN oversight

Sosena Mekuria, RN, provides RN oversight of residents' care plans, including medication-related needs. Delegation may be provided by a delegating RN as each resident's situation requires; it is not assumed that one nurse delegates every task. An RN is typically on site at each home every other day, and trained caregivers provide day-to-day support 24 hours a day. Magnolia does not provide 24-hour on-site nursing.

Good to know

Limits and fit

If a resident needs a nursing task that cannot be delegated and the home cannot otherwise arrange it safely, a different setting may be needed. Medication support is set up individually and written into the resident's care plan.

Questions families ask

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Can any caregiver give my parent their medications?

No. When a resident needs medications administered, it must be done by a legally authorized practitioner or by a qualified caregiver acting under valid nurse delegation for that resident and task, unless another lawful arrangement applies.

FAQ

Can every nursing task be delegated?

No. Washington law limits which tasks can be delegated, and the delegating RN decides case by case whether delegation is appropriate.

FAQ

Who handles refills and medication changes?

Magnolia coordinates with the resident's prescribers and pharmacy as needed and keeps families informed. Prescription changes come from the prescriber.

Services are provided when appropriate for the individual resident, based on assessment, the negotiated care plan, and each home's ability to meet needs safely. This page is general information, not medical advice.
