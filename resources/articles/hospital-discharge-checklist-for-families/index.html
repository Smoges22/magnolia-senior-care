Start with the current care picture

A hospital or rehab stay can change what a loved one needs. Even if someone was managing at home before, discharge may bring new medications, mobility changes, weakness, confusion, wound care, diet instructions, or supervision needs.

Questions for the discharge team

Ask what help is needed with transfers, walking, bathing, toileting, meals, medications, cognition, overnight safety, wound care, oxygen, equipment, and follow-up appointments.

Ask about realistic supervision

Families should ask whether the person can safely be alone and, if so, for how long. This question often clarifies whether home is realistic or whether a structured care setting should be considered.

Gather the documents before placement conversations

Clear documents help care providers respond faster and more accurately. If families are calling Adult Family Homes, assisted living, or home care agencies, it helps to have the basics ready.

Helpful documents

Medication list, discharge summary, diagnoses, therapy notes, mobility status, diet instructions, wound or skin instructions, recent behaviors, POA contact information, and insurance or payment-source details may all be useful.

Care details to summarize

Prepare a simple summary of what happened, what changed, what the timeline is, and what the family is most worried about.

Compare the next care setting calmly

Families often feel pressure when discharge is close. It helps to compare options using the same questions: Can this setting support the current needs? How will communication work? What happens if needs increase?

Adult Family Home questions

Ask about current availability, whether the home can support the discharge needs, medication routines, mobility, memory concerns, meals, bathroom access, and family communication.

Avoid rushed decisions when possible

Urgency is real, but families still deserve clear answers. A private tour or care-fit conversation can reduce uncertainty and prevent mismatched placement.

Frequently asked questions

What information helps Magnolia respond quickly?

Location preference, care needs, medication routine, mobility status, memory concerns, timeline, payment source if known, and family or POA contact information.

Can discharge happen before a tour?

Families should coordinate with the hospital or rehab team and the care provider. A private tour and care-fit conversation are usually important whenever possible.

What if the hospital says discharge must happen soon?

Ask to speak with the discharge planner or social worker, clarify the safest options, request written instructions, and contact care providers quickly with the most accurate information available.

Should families ask about medications?

Yes. Medication changes are common after hospitalization. Ask what changed, why it changed, who manages refills, and what side effects or follow-up should be watched.
